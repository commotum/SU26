Module 1 — Discussion Reflection: WHW 1 🕓 0.25 hour

Purpose

Discussion Reflections are a research-based technique to improve students' conceptual understanding of the material and problems-solving processes (more reflective exercises). This is a way for you to author material that is specific to you and/or your solutions.  For this discussion board, please share brief responses to the following prompts.  Remember that this is an academic setting, so appropriate language and complete sentences are expected. 

Instructions

Review the Written Homework Key and compare your solutions to the solutions posted.  Post written feedback to address each of the actions below: WHW1-Key-1.pdf
Actions

Important: Did not finish or hand in the WHW? You can still complete the reflections for full credit based on your reflection of just looking at the key. 

Action 1: Share one study strategy you used over the last week that you found VERY useful.

Action 2: Comment on two other study strategies that students posted.

Action 3: Complete one of the two options below:

If you had one or more mistakes:
Write out a description of one important mistake that you made.
Write a description of the steps you could have taken to correct the solution.

Example

When I factored out an x, I forgot to take it out of the 3rd term in the sum.
If I had pulled out the x, then I could have divided x to both sides. This would have allowed me to simplify the problem to take the derivative.
If you had all correct solutions, discuss the problem that you found the most challenging:
Summarize the problem statement. 
Then write out and number each step you took to solve the problem. Be as detailed as you can about your solution process. 

Example

Needed to determine if a point satisfies an equation.
1) Cancelled like factors using algebra. 2) Identified the (x, y) coordinate. 3) Plugged in the values to confirm both sides were equal.
Due Date

Friday of week 2 at 11:59 pm Pacific Time

Grading

This activity is worth 15 points (5 points per action).

1.  The most useful study strategy I used over the last week was making sure to review all of the material thoroughly and taking careful notes.  It made it very helpful for when I wasn't sure how to do a certain homework problem to be able to refer back to them and review easily.  I'll also be able to just turn the notes into flashcards for further review throughout the term.

2. I liked Jessalyn's strategy of doing some math everyday.  I would like to try doing some work on problems every day as I feel like it will help me retain things better, as opposed to doing more work at once on fewer days.  The second strategy I like is William's strategy of trying to do at least one warmup problem without any notes.  I think this would help force me to recall information.

3. I did end up getting all of the problems right, but I think the most difficult problem was 1.1 number 27.  The problem said to express the endpoint sums in sigma notation without evaluating, given R100 for ln⁡𝑥 on [1,𝑒].  The form for right-endpoint sums in sigma notation is 𝑛∑𝑖=1 𝑓⁡(𝑥𝑖)Δ𝑥.  We know that Δx = ((b - a)/ n), and the interval is [1, e].  So a = 1, b = e.  We are asked for right end point approximation based on R, with n = 100 intervals.  So Δx = ((e - 1) / 100).  Plug in our values to get 100 ∑i=1 ln(xi) * ((e-1)/100).  The last step is to determine what xi is, which is simply the x value of the right end point of the first interval.  Starting from the beginning of the interval is at 1.  The right endpoint is a single interval's length away from the start of the interval, so 1 + iΔx, where i is the number of the interval.  So our xi = 1 + i((e-1)/100).

 

Plugging xi into the equation and factoring, we get ((e-1)/100) * (100 ∑i=1 ln(1 + i(e-1)/100)).
