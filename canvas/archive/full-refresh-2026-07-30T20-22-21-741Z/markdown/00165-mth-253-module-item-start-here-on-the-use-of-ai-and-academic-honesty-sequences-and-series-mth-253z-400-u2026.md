Start Here: On the use of AI and academic honesty

 

General Goals for the Course

In this course, our goal is to learn the basic theory of sequences and series and to develop the competency and intuition you'll need for the next steps in your math education.

MTH253 sits in the middle of the calculus sequence. Without a solid grasp of this material, it becomes difficult to succeed later in the sequence or in more advanced courses such as MTH311 and MTH312.

Your Responsibilities in Achieving Your Goals

This course is carefully designed to help you build key competencies in sequences and series within the broader context of calculus. Its online format offers more flexibility and personal control than an in-person setting would. In exchange, you have to use that flexibility responsibly.

If you rely on easily available solutions written by someone else for your homework or quiz problems, you're not just engaging in academic dishonesty but you also risk getting little of value from the course, even if you get a good grade. That's wasted time and money, especially since this course is a prerequisite for what comes next.

AI Tools and Math Education

In an online class, it's tempting to use AI tools or lean on classmates for quick answers instead of genuinely working through the material on homework, quizzes, and exams. AI tools have become sophisticated enough that they're now hard to avoid in online searches and hard to block on online exams, even with proctoring in place.

AI is increasingly useful in mathematical research: as a quick reference, a tool for building numerical simulations, or an aid for brainstorming possible approaches to a research problem. But at the level of this course, using it is counterproductive outside a few narrow, specially designed exercises. I'd encourage you to watch
Terence Tao's talk on this subject.

The Use of AI Policy

In MTH253, AI is not allowed on quizzes or exams. Both are deliberately low-stakes and exist as checkpoints to help you gauge your understanding and identify where you need more help.

We also strongly discourage using AI on homework. Any written work you submit must be your own, not copied from someone or somewhere else.

Seeking Help

If you get stuck on a homework or quiz problem, I'm happy to meet one-on-one during virtual office hours or by appointment to help you catch up and work through difficult topics.

For Web Homework specifically, it can be useful to consult AI occasionally, but only after you've struggled with a problem on your own and need a little help understanding where you went wrong. Even more useful, though, is posting your questions on our Q&A forums and answering other students' questions: this tends to pay off more than AI assistance.

Posting on Q&A Discussion Forums

I especially encourage you to post questions about difficult homework problems in the corresponding unit's Q&A Discussions forum, and to stay active replying to others, even if you have partial ideas. Whether you've already solved a problem or are still stuck, reading and posting on the forums is worthwhile: you'll almost always discover something new. Building a friendly learning community is an essential part of this course.
