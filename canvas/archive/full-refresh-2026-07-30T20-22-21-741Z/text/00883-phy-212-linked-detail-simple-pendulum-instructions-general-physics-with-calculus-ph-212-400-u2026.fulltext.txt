    In this experiment, you will investigate damping and frequency in a simple pendulum by recording its motion and analyzing the resulting data using Vernier Video Analysis. A simple pendulum consists of a mass suspended from a string that oscillates back and forth under the influence of gravity. You will record a video of the pendulum’s motion and use video analysis software to track its angular displacement (or horizontal position) as a function of time. From this data, you will determine how the amplitude changes during successive oscillations and extract the damping constant by analyzing the decay of the motion.
 
Build the setup

Build a simple pendulum in the same way as in Labs 3 and 4. Note: the string length must be at least 0.7 m (2 ft) to minimize noticeable precession (i.e., rotation of the plane of oscillation).
Pull the pendulum to the side by a small angle (~5°) and release it.
Record a video of the motion from the front. Please note the following requirements:

Camera stability: Place your camera or phone on a stable surface or stand. Holding the camera by hand will introduce significant measurement errors.
Camera alignment: The camera must be perpendicular to the plane of motion of the pendulum to avoid perspective distortion. This is critical for accurate video analysis.
Recording duration: Approximately 1 minute and above is recommended. You do not need to wait for the pendulum to come completely to rest.
Frame rate: A frame rate of 30 fps or 60 fps is sufficient. 



Evaluate how strongly damped your pendulum is. If it continues swinging for more than 30 seconds with little noticeable decrease in amplitude, consider increasing the air resistance by attaching a larger surface-area object to the pendulum bob, such as a coffee filter, piece of cardstock, or cardboard. A lightly damped pendulum may require a long recording to show a measurable decrease in amplitude. As a useful baseline, aim for a setup in which the amplitude decreases noticeably within approximately 30 seconds after the pendulum is released from an initial angle of about 5°.






Example: The photos below show one possible pendulum setup. The pendulum bob is a mason jar with coins inside to increase its mass and masking tape around the bottom to make the bob easier to identify during video analysis. To increase the damping, lightweight Styrofoam sheets were temporarily taped to both sides of the jar, increasing the surface area and air resistance while having little effect on the mass.

Video Preparation in Vernier Video Analysis

Access Vernier Video Analysis: Address: https://videoanalysis.app/Links to an external site.. (License key: vaky7TWJA7) 
Click Import Video and load your recording.
Set the scale: On the left sidebar, click System → Scale. Set the two ends of the scale bar to the two ends of the string, then enter the string length (in meters) in the input box at the top
Set the origin: On the left sidebar, click System → Origin and move the origin of the coordinate system to the pivot point of the pendulum. Play the entire video to check whether the pivot point remains aligned with the origin. If the pivot point shifts in later frames, you will need to retake the video and ensure the camera set up is stable.
Switch to the polar system: While in the origin setup interface, click the POLAR button at the top to switch from the Cartesian coordinate system to the polar coordinate system. You should see additional columns, including θ (rad), appear in the data table.
Set the axis orientation: The positive x-direction should point directly downward and pass through the rest position of the pendulum. Drag the x circle on the x-axis to adjust its orientation. Play the video to ensure the pendulum oscillates about the x-axis.

 
Analyze the video
The instructions below show the process of manual tracking only. Autotracking requires high-quality recordings with a clear view of the pendulum mass at all times. Please use with caution.

Start tracking: Click the Add button in the top-left corner.
Play the video until the pendulum reaches its first maximum displacement. Use the step forward/backward buttons at the bottom to locate the exact frame.
Mark the position of the pendulum bob at each maximum, equilibrium, and minimum position—that is, when the bob is at the far right, center, and far left, as shown in the practice data set below.

Play the video until the pendulum reaches one of these positions.
Use the step-forward and step-backward buttons to identify the exact frame.
Click on the pendulum bob to record its position. A new entry will appear in the data table on the right, and a corresponding point will appear on the graph.
Continue through the video and repeat this process each time the bob reaches the far right, center, or far left.


Repeat this process for the entire video.
At the upper left, click the menu icon and select Export...CSV to download the data table. Alternatively, copy and paste the data table in the bottom-right corner into an Excel file and proceed to data processing.

 
Extracting the Damping Factor and Frequency
Import and plot the data

Open Vernier graphical analysis Pro versionLinks to an external site. (license key: gaTFp6hnZq) in your browser. From the home screen, under New Experiment select Manual Entry.

At any point during your analysis, you can save your experiment as a .gambl file. This allows you to reopen the experiment later and continue where you left off or share the file with your group members so they can view or continue the analysis.


Locate the empty data table on the right-hand side
Paste the data of time and  directly into the table in Vernier Graphical Analysis
Ensure the data are entered into the correct columns (e.g., x and y)
You should see a damped sinusoidal signal whose amplitude decreases over time now plotted.

 
Extract the Damping Coefficient and Frequency

Make sure you are in the Pro mode of Graphical Analysis before creating a custom fit

Custom Curve Fits is only available in the Pro mode of Graphical Analysis. If your copy of Graphical Analysis isn’t already upgraded, upgrade your copy to Pro.
Click the three dots in the top right corner of the program and click “About”
Enter the license key: gaTFp6hnZq
If prompted, DO NOT try to update the program.


Select and drag the relevant data you would like to fit in Graphical Analysis, or leave it unselected to use all the data
Select “Apply Curve Fit” from the menu that appears or by clicking on the graph symbol in the lower left corner.
Enter the model equation to try using mathematical notation.

For example y= would be entered as A*exp(x)+B*x-C*sin(x).
Note that your independent variable must be referred to as x in your equation.


Graphical Analysis will find the best values of each parameter (in this case A, B, & C) that fit your model to the data!
We can show the uncertainty in the fit parameters by clicking the gear that appears in the box. Very helpful!
