Intro

Here we further develop our understanding of Riemann sums by exploring various approximations and application problems. Riemann sums covered so far include the following: upper sum, lower sum, left endpoint, right endpoint, and midpoint approximations.  The material below will help us develop a deeper understanding of the topic.

Interactive Visualization (Optional)

The Geogebra interactive is a useful tool to explore how these sums apply to different functions. 

Instructions:

Go to Illustration of Definite Integral
Links to an external site.
Graph a function and drag points on x-axis to set bounds.
Adjust the number of rectangles used for a sum approximation with n-slider.
Visualize left (d=0) or right Riemann (d=1) sums over that interval by adjusting d-slider.
Check the boxes to compare actual area with estimates.
Note: Trapezium is only to show other types of sums that we will not be exploring. This will not be on any assessments.  

As you interact with the object, consider the following question:

What type of approximation do we get by setting the d-slider to d = 0.5 ?
