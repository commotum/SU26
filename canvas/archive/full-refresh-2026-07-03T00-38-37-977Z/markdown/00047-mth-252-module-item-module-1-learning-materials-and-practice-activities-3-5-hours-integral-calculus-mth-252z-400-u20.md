Intro

Riemann sums are conceptually easy to understand, but there are subtle details needed in order to make the idea rigorous. Below you will find tools and resources to support your learning and conceptualization of various Riemann sums.

Videos

These YouTube videos are useful resources to review the definitions needed to identify and compute the rectangular areas for our approximations.

About the YouTube Videos: This quarter you will be watching many content videos primarily from three YouTube channels: MathIsPower4u, Krista King, and The Organic Chemistry Tutor. All three of these content providers put out quality material, along with using clear explanations that will set students up for success.  Their videos are reputable and have been well used for years.  Roughly speaking, the videos from MathIsPower4u and Krista King will be shorter videos introducing and discussing concepts,  whereas videos from The Organic Chemistry Tutor tend to be longer showcasing the author working through a variety of problems on a given topic.  It is very important to note that they are teaching the general concept, and at any point, if you have any questions or would like further explanation for a very specific problem from your class, please reach out to your instructor.  We hope you will enjoy this quarter's video playlist.

Interactive Visualization (Optional)

The Geogebra interactive is a useful tool to explore how these sums apply to different functions. 

Instructions:

Go to GeoGebra Riemann Sums
Links to an external site.
Graph a function over a set of adjustable bounds
Visualize left or right Riemann sums over that interval
Adjust the number of rectangles used for a sum approximation
Evaluate a left or right Riemann sum
See the exact area
Adjust any of the sliders, select the boxes, or change the function. 

As you interact with the object, consider the following questions:

Does the left sum always give you a lower and the right an upper sum approximation?
Does this remain true using the function 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑥^3−10𝑥+3 and adjusting the left bound to x = -2 ?
Relative to the actual area under the curve, what happens to the area of the rectangles as n → ∞ ?
