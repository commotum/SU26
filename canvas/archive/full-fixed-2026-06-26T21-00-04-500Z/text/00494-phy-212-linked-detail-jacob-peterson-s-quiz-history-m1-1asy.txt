Rotational motion is similar to regular linear motion, but instead of position, velocity, and acceleration, we use angle, angular velocity, and angular acceleration. Angular velocity tells how fast something spins, while angular acceleration tells how quickly that spinning changes. When a disk slows to a stop, its angular acceleration points opposite its angular velocity. The right-hand rule helps determine whether angular velocity or angular acceleration points into or out of the page. The total angle turned can be found using rotational kinematics, then converted from radians to revolutions.
