Catalog Course Description
Extends and adapts models based on forces and energy to describe and quantify the fundamental
physical phenomena of rotational motion, oscillatory motion, collective motion, and optics.
Explores how multiple representations, mathematical sensemaking, and physical explanations
strengthen the construction and deployment of the models. Investigates the methods by which
physics is built through experiment and observation.

Course Specific Measurable Student Learning Outcomes
1. Apply Newton’s laws and conservation to analyze the behavior of systems with
rotational, oscillatory, or wavelike motion.
2. Construct a variety of representations, including graphs of related motion
quantities, extended free-body diagrams, and symbolic equations, and translate from
one representation to another.
3. Explain physical behavior based on observations, Newton’s laws, and conservation
principles.
4. Investigate the physical world to test models and draw conclusions using the
scientific method.
5. Explain how physics principles have led to the development of real-world
inventions and devices and how these inventions and devices have impacted society.
Your learning will be assessed using a wide variety of assignments. Most assignments
in this class are formative. Your responses to the pre-lecture assignments,
participation assignments and homework activities will serve a primary formative role
in how you learn physics. Your learning will also be assessed by a term project, labs,
quizzes and a cumulative final exam for which you will also receive detailed feedback.
Expectations for Time and Participation
Participation is essential and expected in all components of this course. Attendance
in live Zoom lectures is optional, with corresponding asynchronous alternatives
available for every synchronous assignment.
This course combines approximately 120 total hours of instruction, online activities,
and assignments for 4 credits. This means that, according to OSU policy, students
are expected to work on course activities (readings, labs, homework, discussions,
assessments, etc.) for approximately 12 hours per week in this 4-credit course.
Please plan out your schedule to carve out at least 12 hours each week to
complete the activities in this course.
This course is asynchronous and somewhat flexible, but not self-paced. Our schedule
of Course Content and the due dates that appear in Canvas provide guidelines for
how you’ll interact and with what frequency. We recommend that you create your
own workload schedule and set reminders for assignment due dates.

technical issues, weather, travel, and any other excused reasons. You can

Canvas.
